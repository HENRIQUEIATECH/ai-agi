GENERAL AI and its Base

A Philosophical and Technical Manifesto on the Crisis of Abstraction and the Return to Silicon Rigor
Addressed to: OpenAI, MIT, Global Community of Programmers and Computer Scientists

Dear Colleagues, Researchers, and Engineers,

I write this message strictly through the lens of the Philosophy of Computer Science. At this historical turning point, where the pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) mobilizes the world's leading laboratories—from OpenAI to MIT—it is necessary to descend from the ivory tower of high-level abstractions and face an inconvenient truth: the foundations upon which we are building the future of intelligence are malleable, inefficient, and ontologically flawed.

The industry has grown accustomed to celebrating the gigantism of models and superficial development speed, while ignoring the silent collapse of the underlying infrastructure. To make modern language models viable, a technological patchwork sustained by hypertrophied ecosystems like Python and Java was erected. Data, which should flow freely and surgically through hardware registers, is trapped in infinite layers of wrappers, virtual environments, and heavy interpreters. The manifest result is what I define as computational fat.

The Illusion of Floating Abstractions

In traditional interpreted and object-oriented languages, the simplicity of a mathematical operation conceals a catastrophic waste of resources. An integer no longer represents mere bytes allocated in memory; it carries dense structures of metadata, reference counters, and dynamic type checks. Multiply this inefficiency by the billions of parameters of a modern AI and the petabytes of processed data, and we find the exact explanation for the astronomical energy costs and performance fluctuations that currently suffocate the largest server infrastructures on the planet.

"Contemporary software engineering has forgotten silicon. By distancing the programmer from the machine's physical reality, a generation of systems has been created that mimics intelligence but fails in the elementary mathematics of efficiency."

The ambition to generate complex and superior code through AI stumbles upon the same vice of its origin. Trained on the redundant and poorly optimized code that floods the public internet, current neural networks perpetuate the mentality of lazy abstraction. Present-day AI predicts the next word via statistical probability but lacks the rigid logical reasoning necessary to architect integrated and self-sufficient software systems."

The Return to a Solid Base: Occam's Razor in the C Language

The structural salvation of Artificial Intelligence does not reside in creating a new commercial framework or stacking new automation tools. The answer demands a strict return to the simple and indestructible base of computing: the purism of the C Language.

In C, illusion gives way to reality. There are no floating automatic memory managers (Garbage Collectors) acting unpredictably, nor software black boxes masking bottlenecks. Every assignment, every pointer, and every memory allocation constitutes a direct mathematical decision, perfectly synchronized with the physical architecture of the processor. Groundbreaking projects emerging at the vanguard of technology—by rewriting inference engines and training fully in pure C and CUDA, entirely eliminating dependency on PyTorch—empirically prove that removing software fat allows massive models to operate with extreme speed and at a fraction of the cost.

For Artificial Intelligence to evolve toward real autonomy and super-coding capability, its linear algebra algorithms must talk directly to the chips, free of intermediaries. We must rescue rigor and absolute control over the bit.

Conclusion

The artificial complexity of floating languages has exhausted its cycle of economic and energetic tolerance. If we intend to achieve a robust, sustainable, and truly brilliant General Intelligence, the path requires a radical debugging of our base. It is time to abandon volatile code and rebuild computer science upon the firm rock of solid-base programming.
